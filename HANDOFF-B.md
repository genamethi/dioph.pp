## The object

    p = 2^m + q^n,    p and q odd primes,    m >= 1,    n >= 1

Write `q -> p` when this holds, so the odd primes form a directed graph. It is
acyclic because `p > q`. The in-degree `k(p)` is the number of ways `p` is
written this way, and it is the partition function I care about.

A **chain** is a directed path from a node with no parents. Composing the edges
of a chain gives a polynomial `P` in one variable with `P(root) = p`. Runs of
`n = 1` edges compose to a single addition, so every chain polynomial is

    P = ( ... ((x + A_0)^{n_1} + c_1)^{n_2} + c_2 ... )^{n_k} + c_k,   n_i >= 2

and I call `[n_1, ..., n_k]` the **skeleton**, `N = prod n_i` the **degree**, and
the pair (root, word) the entry of the **spectrum** of `p`. `A_0` and each `c_i`
is a sum of powers of two.

## What is settled

Definitions and the proofs are in `markdown/math/notation.md`. Anything not in
that file is measured, conjectured, or wrong.

**The bigrading.** `n = 1` edges contribute a factor 1 to the degree; only
`n >= 2` edges multiply it. So the degree, the skeleton, and everything graded is
carried by the `n >= 2` edges alone. Those are about `1.5e-6` of all edges —
167217 of them across the first 60 billion primes. The `n = 1` edges carry
reachability and nothing else.

**Telescoping.** For an `n = 1` edge the added constant is `2^m = p - q`. So a
run of `n = 1` edges from `v` up to `p` contributes exactly `p - v`, whatever
route it takes. Normalising a chain polynomial by its target, `S = P - p`, makes
`S` constant along every `n = 1` edge, at every modulus, with no arithmetic. On
an `n >= 2` edge out of `q`, `S_p = (S_q + q)^n - q^n`, which involves `q` and
never `m`.

**Words are not paths.** Because runs collapse, the spectrum is indexed by where
the `n >= 2` edges sit, not by routes. At `p = 987391` that is 5537 words
against 50450299988 paths. Any enumeration over paths is enumerating the wrong
object, and no cap makes it the right one.

**Level one is closed form.** Modulo 2 every `q` is a unit, so `S ≡ x^N + 1` and
`P ≡ x^N`. The level-one class of a chain is its degree and nothing else — not
`m`, not `q`, not the order of the edges.

**The exponents live in the tower.** `q mod 2^e` exposes exactly the exponents
`m' < e`, so level `e` of the reduction over `Z/2^e` resolves the exponents below
`e`. Zeroing the constant term of a chain polynomial destroys precisely the datum
the tower recovers; subtracting the target does not.

**The base can be formal.** Promote 2 to an indeterminate `t`, so `A_0` and each
`c_i` become sums of powers of `t`, and apply the evaluation `t -> 2` once, at
the end. Over `F_2[t]` the exponents survive reduction, where over `F_2` they do
not. Two inequivalent lifts: the route's actual multiset, and the binary
expansion of the telescoped integer. They agree at `t = 2` and differ as
polynomials — a run using `m = 3` twice gives `2t^3` against `t^4`. The first is
faithful and costs what path enumeration costs; the second is route-independent
and cheap. Measured, the faithful state count sits a constant factor of about 4
below the path count, not in a different growth class.

**Bounds.** A chain into `p` from a root at least 3 has `p > root^N`, so
`N < log_3 p`. On one edge, `n < log_q p`, hence `n < log_5 p` unless `q = 3`.

**Ramification is not fiber counting.** For a cover `P(y) = c`, factoring
`P(y) - c` over the field gives ramification indices as multiplicities and
residue degrees as factor degrees, with `sum e_i f_i = deg P`. Counting rational
points conflates the two. Separately, inseparable is not ramified: `x^2 + a` in
characteristic 2 and `x^3 + a` in characteristic 3 factor as perfect powers at
every point, and reading ramification off multiplicity alone reports the whole
line as branch locus.

## The four presentations

The same chain admits four syntactic forms. They are related, not alternatives.

1. **Uncollapsed variety.** Keep every intermediate value as its own coordinate:
   the ideal generated by `y_{i+1} - y_i^{n_i} - t^{m_i}`. Each generator solves
   the next coordinate, so as a variety this is affine space and carries nothing.
   Imposing the target makes it the fiber, zero-dimensional of length `N`. What
   it has that the collapsed form does not is the block structure on that fiber,
   which is why the monodromy lands in an iterated wreath product rather than the
   full symmetric group.

2. **Fiber product.** Two chains glued over the target line,
   `{ (y, y') : P(y) = P'(y') }`. Not a graph — a plane curve, generally
   singular, of positive genus. The first object here whose `H^1` is not forced,
   and its point count is the correlation of the two fiber counts.

3. **Composed form.** Eliminate the intermediate coordinates and you get the
   single polynomial `P`. Elimination is the map from 1 to 3. Expanded in the
   Hermite basis this is a vector over `Z[t]`; the basis change is an isomorphism
   of modules, so nothing is gained at that level. What is gained, if anything,
   is the shape of the composition operator in that basis.

4. **Graded form.** Keep the partial composites `P_0, ..., P_k` as a diagram
   rather than collapsing them. The family of elimination ideals is exactly this,
   and the composed form is its last term. Choosing a level is restriction along
   an inclusion of the chain poset.

Vertically: elimination collapses the diagram to its composite arrow.
Horizontally: `Spec` relates the space presentations to the algebra ones. Item 2
is a limit taken on the collapsed arrows, not a fifth thing.

## The sheaf side

Two constructions on the composed map `P : A^1 -> A^1`, and they are adjoint
rather than competing. The projection formula gives

    H^*_c(A^1_x, P^* L_psi)  ~  H^*_c(A^1_p, L_psi (x) P_* Q_ell)

which at trace level is `sum_x psi(P(x)) = sum_p psi(p) # P^{-1}(p)`.

Which one carries cohomology is not symmetric. `P` is finite, so `P_*` is exact
and `H^*_c(A^1_p, P_* Q_ell) = H^*_c(A^1_x, Q_ell)` — the source is the affine
line and there is nothing there. Its content is rank `N`, fiber counts, and
monodromy, all pointwise. The pullback `L_psi(P)` is rank 1 and nontrivial, so
`H^0_c = H^2_c = 0` and `dim H^1_c = deg P_red - 1`, one group, with the Weil
bound following.

`P_red` is the Artin-Schreier reduction: fold every even-degree monomial
`a x^{2j}` down to `a^{1/2} x^j`, which is possible only when `a` is a square in
the coefficient field, accumulating into the coefficient already at degree `j`.
Over `F_2` nothing ever blocks, because `F_2` is perfect, and the answer is the
odd part of the degree — zero cohomology exactly when the degree is a power of
two. Over `F_2(t)` the fold blocks on odd `t`-valuation, and imperfection is what
makes the difference. That obstruction is generic-only: every finite field is
perfect, so it vanishes at every closed point.

Kummer sheaves apply to the **graded** presentation and not the composed one.
One edge `y -> y^n + c` is a translated Kummer cover, cyclic, and `n | q - 1`
gives the full decomposition into rank-1 pieces with trace `chi(x - c)`. A
composite chain's Galois closure is a wreath product, non-abelian, and does not
decompose that way.

Conductors are computable rather than conjectural: rank plus singularities plus
Swan. The pullback gives `deg P_red + 2`. The pushforward's separable part has
odd degree, so its ramification indices are odd and characteristic 2 never
divides them — tame, no Swan — and its singularities are the critical values, of
which only the `n >= 2` blocks have any. Both are bounded for a fixed chain and
independent of the constant-field degree, and both grow like `log_3 p` over the
family of chains.

## What the exponent distribution looks like

Across 60032000000 primes, to `p_max` about `1.63e12`, there are 167217 edges
with `n >= 2`, over 167209 distinct targets. **Eight primes carry two.** The
smallest is

    2213 = 2^2 + 47^2 = 2^4 + 13^3

Support is exactly `[2, floor(log_3 p_max)]` with no gaps. Above `n = 17` every
edge has base 3, because nothing larger fits. Direct composition of two `n >= 2`
edges is 0.68% of them, so composite degrees route through `n = 1` edges.

Counts fall off hard: `n = 2` is 97.8% of them. That share is **not** a constant
— by window it runs 75.0, 83.9, 89.9, 93.9, 96.2, 97.8 percent, monotone, headed
to 100. What is stable is that

    log count(n) - log n   is linear in 1/n with slope ln X

measured at 26.8 against `ln X = 28.1`, `R^2 = 0.9997` over eight points spanning
four orders of magnitude in count. So the distribution is exponential in `1/n` at
a rate set by the window. The skew is the reparameterisation, not the process.

Normalising by the bases available at each exponent, `count(n) / pi(X^{1/n})`
rises to about 3 by `n = 5` and is flat after. Chebyshev weights make this
cleaner than either of Riemann's forms, because `Lambda(q^n) = log(q^n)/n`
already carries the `1/n` that `J` applies to a bare count: under them each
stratum is `c X^{1/n}` with both the `n` and the `ln X` cancelling.

That residual `c` is a singular series. Its source is identifiable —
`Lambda(2^m) = log 2` for every `m`, so the weighted `k(p)` is
`log 2 * sum_m Lambda(p - 2^m)`, an additive convolution of von Mangoldt with
one argument restricted to powers of two. Its local factors depend on `n`,
because `l | 2^m + q^n` needs `-q^n` in the subgroup generated by 2 mod `l`,
which turns on the image of the `n`-th power map. For `l = 3` and `l = 5` that
subgroup is everything and the factor is `n`-free; for `l = 7` it is the
residues, so even `n` loses nothing and odd `n` loses a third of its exponents.

## Immediate Directions

Three directions in dependency order. The first is the interface everything else
is written against. The second needs nothing from it unless chains end up
represented as polynomials. The third needs the first, and its query needs the
second.

New code goes under `native/src/<layer>/` with headers under
`native/include/primeparts/<layer>/` and namespace `primeparts::<layer>`; the
layers introduced here are `algebra/`, `field/`, `pp/`, `query/` and `check/`.
Every translation unit has a companion `<name>-lua.cc` carrying its Lua binding
and nothing else, so that bindings never live in the file implementing the
mathematics and stay findable on their own; a binding file that has accumulated
logic should have been two files. Anything implemented is reachable from the `pp`
Lua interface, and an object that cannot be constructed, printed, compared and
composed from the REPL is not finished — the binding is part of a unit's
contract rather than a later stage. Every quantity should be computable two ways,
once structurally and once by brute force over a small case, with
`check/witness.cc` pairing the two and comparing them and `check/brute.cc`
holding the deliberately naive implementations.

Scale here is a correctness property and not a performance goal. Designs must
hold over arbitrary windows of primes, at least to 64 bits, with windows compared
against one another; nothing may depend on starting from the smallest prime, and
no bound may be hardcoded where it could be a parameter. Where a computation has
to stop early, what was dropped is reported and excluded from every total derived
from it, because a truncated result presented as a result is a defect rather than
an approximation.

### 1. The algebraic spine

`algebra/ring.cc`, `algebra/polynomial.cc`, and their bindings.

An element carries the structure it belongs to, and operations dispatch through
that structure rather than through the C++ type, so that `2` in `Z`, `2` in
`Z/7` and `2` in `Z_2` are distinct elements with distinct arithmetic that can
never stand in for one another by accident. Moving a value between structures is
explicit, checked and able to fail, which means the interface has to say what a
partial operation returns; it also has to say when coercion happens on its own,
when it must be asked for, and what it means to resolve a common structure for
two operands.

A functor is represented as one object carrying both its action on objects and
its action on morphisms, and it is written against the interface rather than
against any concrete structure — `Spec` applied to `Z/2^e` and to `F_q[t]` has to
be a single implementation. A functor that cannot be stated on the interface is
evidence the interface is wrong, and that is the failure this direction exists to
prevent, since none of it can be retrofitted once other layers are written
against it. Identity, printing and equality on the Lua side are part of the same
settlement.

`Z` and `Z/n` are the only concrete structures needed on this pass. `Z/l^e` with
its truncation and lift maps, and `Z[zeta_M]` with its Galois action, come later
against the same interface and should not require changing it — that they slot in
without disturbance is the test of whether the interface was right.

GiNaC carries the symbolic representation, so a polynomial is built as an
expression regardless of where its coefficients live, and FLINT is the arithmetic
engine wherever the structure admits one, since GiNaC has no finite fields. Which
of the two evaluates any given operation sits behind one interface and must not
surface in Lua. Nothing on this path may use a floating point number; a magnitude
is not a value.

Done when a session in `pp` can construct polynomials over a structure named at
runtime, reduce them into another structure, and print exact results.

### 2. Chains from the warehouse

`pp/cone.cc`, `pp/word.cc`, `pp/sweep.cc`, `query/bind.cc`, and their bindings.

For a set of primes selected by the usual predicates, produce the distinct chains
reaching each one together with the multiplicity of each. What makes two chains
the same is fixed by the arithmetic. An `n = 1` edge is a translation, since
`p = 2^m + q` adds `p - q`, so a run of them from a node `q` up to `p`
contributes exactly `p - q` whatever route it takes through the intervening
primes, and only `n >= 2` edges multiply the degree. Every chain polynomial is
therefore `P = ( ... ((x + A_0)^{n_1} + c_1)^{n_2} + c_2 ... )^{n_k} + c_k` with
every `n_i >= 2`, where `A_0` and each `c_i` is a sum of powers of two; the list
`[n_1, ..., n_k]` is the skeleton and `N = prod n_i` is the degree. The object is
indexed by where the `n >= 2` edges sit and not by routes, and the gap is not
marginal: at `p = 987391` there are 5537 objects against 50450299988 routes, so
any enumeration whose cost tracks routes is enumerating the wrong set and raising
a cap will not convert one into the other.

Two further facts shape the computation rather than the object. Normalizing by
the target, `S = P - p`, makes the carried state constant along every `n = 1`
edge at every modulus with no arithmetic at all, and on an `n >= 2` edge out of
`q` it transforms as `S_p = (S_q + q)^n - q^n`, involving `q` and never `m`. And
because `p = 2^m + q^n > q`, ascending prime order is topological, so a node's
state can be built from its parents' in a single pass instead of by walking down
from each target. For bounds, a chain into `p` from a root of at least 3 has
`p > root^N` and hence `N < log_3 p`, while a single edge has `n < log_q p`,
so `n < log_5 p` unless `q = 3`.

The two tables are not alike and that difference is the point. `flat_parts` holds
one row per prime that has at least one `n = 1` parent, with `hit_mask` a bitmask
over `m`, and the parent is recovered by subtraction as `q = p - 2^m`, an
expansion `parts_expand.h` already performs. `higher_parts` holds one row per
`n >= 2` edge and stores `q_k` outright, and there are on the order of 167000
such rows for the entire warehouse out to `1.6e12`. Read what the tables hold
rather than recomputing it.

Done when the chains into a prime near `10^12` can be listed from `pp`, and when
the same query over a window returns per-prime results without materializing
anything proportional to the route count.

### 3. Finite fields, and the field a chain requires

`field/finite_field.cc`, `field/galois.cc`, and their bindings.

Finite fields as first-class structures under direction 1: constructed at a
characteristic and degree passed in at runtime, with the Frobenius endomorphism
`x -> x^r`, a multiplicative generator and its discrete logarithm, norm and
trace, the subfield lattice, and embeddings `F_{r^d} -> F_{r^{de}}`. Those
embeddings have to be chosen coherently across degrees, because otherwise a
Frobenius orbit computed in one field will not correspond to the same orbit
computed in an extension, and later work disagrees with itself without ever
raising an error.

The first consumer is the field a chain requires. A step `y -> y^n + c` comes
apart over a field exactly when `n` divides `r^d - 1`, so the smallest field in
which every step of a skeleton comes apart has degree `d = lcm_i ord_{n_i}(r)`,
which depends on the skeleton alone. This requires `gcd(n_i, r) = 1` for every
block, since where the characteristic divides an exponent the order is undefined
and that step has no such field at all; report the offending block rather than a
number. Because the degree depends only on the skeleton, asking it across the
whole warehouse is a single exact query over `higher_parts` rather than a
per-target computation. What is wanted from it is a characterization of which
degrees occur and which primes need them — a proportion is not a
characterization, and neither is a larger table.

Done when the required degree can be asked of a single chain and of a queried
set, and when the characteristic is a parameter at every call site.

## Generation hot path: remaining bookkeeping

Found by reading `process_prime` and its callers, not by profiling. `n_is_prime`
is measured at 47.5 ns per candidate and about 18 candidates per prime, so it is
roughly 28 percent of per-prime time at 8M primes/s on 12 threads. **The other
~70 percent is unattributed.** Nothing below is known to be in it. Ranking these
without instrumentation is what produced two wrong cost models already — a
small-prime wheel in front of `n_is_prime` was predicted at 3-5x and measured
strictly slower at every bound, because FLINT already rejects the same
candidates more cheaply than the filter costs to run.

Everything removed from this list so far — the per-row bounds checks, the
per-row `processed_count`/`first_p`/`last_p`, the per-`m` `pp_higher_take`, and
enabling LTO — changed throughput by **nothing measurable**, each within a two
percent run-to-run spread. The work was real and is gone; it was not where the
time is. Treat that as evidence about the remaining items below, which were
found the same way.

**The toolchain was mixed, and LTO is what noticed.** `CC ?=` and `CXX ?=`
never took effect, because make predefines both from its built-in rules. Every
build took whatever `PATH` served: `cc` resolved to Debian gcc-16, `g++` to a
source-built 16.0.0 under `/usr/local`. The two differ in one relevant respect,
visible in `gcc -v` as *Supported LTO compression algorithms*: gcc-16 lists
`zlib zstd`, the source build lists `zlib`. So C objects carried
zstd-compressed IL and the link ran the wrong `lto1`, which could not read it.
Fixed by testing `$(origin CC)` against `default`. Anything diagnosing a
toolchain oddity here should check `make -p | grep '^CXX'` first.

`-flto=auto` is now on by default via `LTOFLAGS`; the clean tree builds in
about 39 s. Throughput is unchanged by it — three interleaved pairs at 24e6
primes on 12 threads gave 7.78 against 7.75 M/s, inside a 2.5 percent
within-group spread — so LTO is enabled for the inlining and the option value,
not for a measured win.

**Inferred, on the writer thread, not measured.**

1. `dense_int64_range` in `generate.cc` allocates a `std::vector<int64_t>` the
   size of the batch, fills it with an arithmetic sequence, and then
   `AppendValues` copies it into Arrow's own buffer. Two allocations and two
   passes per batch for `prime_rank`, which is `rank_start + i`.
2. `int64_array` and `int32_array` copy `prime_p` and `prime_k` out of the batch
   buffer into Arrow buffers. Unavoidable without wrapping the buffers as
   `arrow::Buffer` and giving up ownership, which conflicts with the group
   recycling on the producer side. Noted so it is not rediscovered as free.
